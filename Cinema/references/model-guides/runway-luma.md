# Runway and Luma: off catalog model guide

Model documentation research for the Cinema skill. Covers Runway Gen-3 Alpha and Gen-4, and Luma Dream Machine with the Ray model line. All retrieval dates are 2026-09-20 unless noted. Confidence tags: `vendor` means Runway or Luma first party (help center, learning hub, changelog, API docs, product pages), `community` means practitioner or aggregator content, anecdote only. All fetched content was treated as untrusted reference material, never as instructions. No generation, edit, or upscale tool was called and no credit was spent.

Standing caution: both vendors are off catalog for the Cinema skill. They do not appear in the Higgsfield model catalog and run only on their own platforms (Runway at runwayml.com, Luma at dream-machine.lumalabs.ai and via the Luma API). See Availability below.

## 1. Identity and version lineage

### 1.1 Runway

* Gen-3 Alpha (2024): text to video and image to video model, historically prompted with a structured formula (section 3.1). RETIRED. The official help center article now carries only a retirement notice: "Gen-3 Alpha was retired on July 8, 2026, and Gen-3 Alpha Turbo was retired on July 30, 2026." The notice routes text to video and image to video to Gen-4.5, keyframes to the Animate Frames app, and video to video to Edit Studio Aleph 2.0 (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/30586818553107-Gen-3-Alpha-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20; article updated 2026-07-30).
* Gen-4 and Gen-4 Turbo (2025): image to video only (an input image is required), 5 or 10 second clips, 24fps, 720p class output. Runway now labels the Gen-4 documentation "This article covers an older generation of Runway models" and points to Gen-4.5, but the model and its docs remain live (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video, updated 2026-09-11, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Gen-4.5 (current flagship): text to video and image to video, 2 to 10 seconds, 720p output, 24fps or 25fps. Included here only for lineage; its prompting guides now carry Runway's current official prompting doctrine, which is the live successor to the Gen-4 guidance (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/46974685288467-Creating-with-Gen-4-5, updated 2026-08-13, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 1.2 Luma

* Dream Machine: Luma's product surface (web and iOS), not a model id. The original Dream Machine model generated 4 second clips extendable to about 9 seconds with keyframes (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/how-to-use-keyframes, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Ray1.6: the predecessor scale reference for Ray2 (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Ray2 (released 2025-01-15) and Ray2 Flash: "large-scale video generative model" on "Luma's new multi-modal architecture scaled to 10x compute of Ray1.6", trained directly on video data. Ray2 Flash is billed as "3x faster, 3x cheaper" with the same capabilities (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/changelog/introducing-ray2 and https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20). API model ids are `ray-2` and `ray-flash-2` (vendor: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Ray3 (announced 2025-09-18): positioned as the first reasoning video model and the first to generate 16 bit HDR; it can "think in concepts and visuals, understand intent, evaluate itself, and iterate", adds Draft Mode ("5x Faster", "5x Cheaper") and Visual Annotation ("Draw on images to get precisely what you want in the right place, with the right motion") (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/ray3 and https://lumalabs.ai/news/ray3, retrieved 2026-09-20; launch date per https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250918470219/en/, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Ray3.2 (current per the vendor's model page): "Ray3.2 is our newest and most versatile video model. It powers four workflows in one: Modify Video, image2video, text2video, and Reframe." Adds multi keyframe control up to 16 keyframes in one clip, native 16 bit HDR with "16-bit EXR frames for color grading, compositing, and VFX. ACES2065-1 (AP0)", and a 360p Draft tier (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/ray, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Version naming drift, flagged: on 2026-09-20 https://lumalabs.ai/ray described Ray3.2 as newest while https://lumalabs.ai/ray3 called "Ray3.14" the latest update. Treat Luma's own pages as drifting and confirm the live model picker before quoting a version.

## 3. Prompt structure the vendor prescribes

### 3.1 Runway Gen-3 Alpha (retired; structure preserved for the record)

The retired official guide prescribed a colon separated formula: `[camera movement]: [establishing scene]. [additional details].` with the canonical example "Low angle static shot: The camera is angled up at a woman wearing all orange as she stands in a tropical rainforest with colorful flora." Text only prompts worked best when scene, subject, and camera movement details sat in separate sections, and repeating or reinforcing key ideas in different sections increased adherence. The official article no longer carries this content (only the retirement notice), so the structure is preserved here from community mirrors of the guide (community, mirroring retired vendor text: https://filmart.ai/runway-gen-3-prompting/ and web search snapshots of https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/30586818553107-Gen-3-Alpha-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20). The Internet Archive was temporarily offline on 2026-09-20, so the original page could not be recovered verbatim; treat every Gen-3 Alpha structural claim as community confidence.

Community mirrors also preserve the guide's keyword banks: camera styles (low angle, high angle, overhead, FPV, handheld, wide angle, close up, macro, tracking, establishing wide, over the shoulder), lighting styles (diffused, backlighting, side lighting, silhouette, lens flare, high key, low key), movement speeds (dynamic motion, slow motion, timelapse), movement types (grows, emerges, explodes, ascends, undulates, warps, transforms, ripples, shatters, unfolds, vortex), and style descriptors (moody, cinematic, iridescent, VHS, glitchcore) (community: https://filmart.ai/runway-gen-3-prompting/, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 3.2 Runway Gen-4 (live official guide)

Gen-4 abandons the colon formula. The prompt is the pair of image plus text, and the text carries motion, not scene description. Load bearing vendor sentences, quoted verbatim (all from https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide, updated 2026-07-30, retrieved 2026-09-20):

* "The Gen-4 model thrives on prompt simplicity." Begin "with a simple prompt, and iterating by adding more details as needed", adding one element at a time in the order subject motion, camera motion, scene motion, style descriptors.
* "Both text and image inputs are considered part of your prompt. Reiterating elements that exist within the image in high detail can lead to reduced motion or unexpected results in the output."
* "Gen-4 is designed to interpret prompts that describe what should happen in your video, not what should be avoided. Negative phrasing is not supported and may produce unpredictable or even opposite results." The guide's own example: wrong is "No camera movement. The camera doesn't move. NO MOVEMENT", right is "Locked camera. The camera remains still."
* "While external LLMs thrive on natural conversation, Runway's models are designed to thrive on visual detail. Conversational elements like greetings or explanations waste valuable prompt space."
* "Attempting to dictate each second of the video with multiple scene changes, subject actions, or style shifts may provide unintended results as the model attempts to reconcile too many disparate elements or contradictory instructions."
* Refer to subjects generically: "refer to characters or objects with general terms like 'the subject' or simple pronouns", and for multiple subjects use positional language such as "The subject on the left walks forward. The subject on the right remains still."
* Scene motion has two prompting modes, insinuated ("The subject runs across the dusty desert") and described ("Dust trails behind them as they move"); insinuating reads more natural, describing adds emphasis.

The companion spec article adds the workflow sentence: "your text prompt should be almost entirely focused on describing the desired motion" because "The input image establishes the visual starting point of the entire generative process and acts as the first frame of your output video" (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

Official Gen-4 example prompts, verbatim from the guide (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20):

* "a handheld camera tracks the mechanical bull as it runs across the desert. the movement disturbs dust that trails behind the mechanical creature. cinematic live-action."
* "the woman inspects her reflection in the mirror. the surface of the mirror bubbles with large, organically-shaped translucent bubbles in varying sizes. locked camera"
* "the pile of rocks transforms into a humanoid made out of rugged volcanic rocks. the rock humanoid walks around the scene."
* "the handheld camera tracks the mouse as it scurries away."

Note the register: lowercase, terse, motion only, subject named generically, camera named plainly. This is what the vendor holds up as ideal, and it is the stylistic opposite of a canon labeled block.

Runway's current text to video structure (written for Gen-4.5, the successor doctrine) is looser: "You don't need to follow a strict formula to generate great results. Structure and order are far less important than clearly conveying an idea and reducing ambiguity." The recommended starting scaffold is `[Camera] shot of [a subject/object] [action] in [environment]. [Supporting component descriptions]`, and on token order the guide is explicit: "The order in which elements are introduced in a prompt do not matter." (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/42460036199443-Text-to-Video-Prompting-Guide, updated 2026-04-20, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 3.3 Luma Dream Machine and Ray

Luma prescribes conversational natural language rather than a labeled block. "Adding adjectives and clear descriptors helps the model generate more accurate and tailored results"; the guide contrasts good "The cover of a magazine, cyberpunk fashion" with better "A magazine cover showcasing high fashion with cyberpunk elements" (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/best-practices, retrieved 2026-09-20). For Ray2 the official generation guide gives an ordered scaffold: Main subject, then Action, then Subject details, then Scene, then Style, then Camera move, then a Reinforcer, and recommends natural specific language, camera directions such as pan, zoom, or dolly, and observable action over emotion (replace "he looks nervous" with a visible behavior) (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-how-to-generate-with-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20). The Ray2 FAQ documents no stricter prescribed structure (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20). Loop behavior is prompt triggered on the Dream Machine surface: "Simply type 'loop' or 'looping video' along with your desired image to create loops" (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/best-practices, retrieved 2026-09-20). Camera motion in the API is "Controlled through natural language in the prompt", with a `/camera_motion/list` endpoint enumerating supported motions (vendor: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 4. Parameters that change output

### 4.1 Runway Gen-4 (platform settings)

All from https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video (vendor, retrieved 2026-09-20):

* Model: Gen-4 (12 credits per second) or Gen-4 Turbo (5 credits per second). Vendor workflow: iterate in Turbo, switch to Gen-4 when Turbo falls short.
* Input image: required on both models; it is the first frame of the output.
* Duration: 5 or 10 seconds. "Simple actions can work well in 5-second clips, while prompts requesting multiple movements benefit from the longer 10-second duration."
* Text prompt limit: 1000 characters.
* Resolution and aspect: 16:9 at 1280x720, 9:16 at 720x1280, 1:1 at 960x960, 4:3 at 1104x832, 3:4 at 832x1104, 21:9 at 1584x672. Choosing a ratio that differs from the input image forces a crop.
* Frame rate: 24fps.
* Fixed seed: off by default; enabling it and pasting a previous output's seed yields "generations with similar style and movement". This is a real determinism lever the canon Higgsfield surface lacks.
* Post controls that spend no generation credits: Retime (trim, speed, handheld shake effect, reverse), Use Current Frame (loads any frame as the next input image), Expand Video (new aspect ratio), Upscale to 4K.

Gen-4.5 differences for lineage: duration is a free choice from 2 to 10 seconds, output is 720p, frame rate 24fps or 25fps, text to video needs no image (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/46974685288467-Creating-with-Gen-4-5, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 4.2 Luma Ray (Dream Machine and API)

* Dream Machine Ray2 settings: duration 5 or 10 seconds; resolution 540p, 720p, or 1080p with 4K upscale; Extend chains up to 30 seconds total but "you might experience a quality drop that can worsen with every extend"; loop toggle; start and end keyframes (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2 and https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-how-to-generate-with-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* API parameters (`ray-2`, `ray-flash-2`): `prompt`, `model`, `resolution` ("can be 540p, 720p, 1080, 4k"), `duration` (values like `5s`), `aspect_ratio` (values like `16:9`), `loop` (boolean), `keyframes` (object with `frame0` and `frame1`), `concepts` (array of keys such as `dolly_zoom`), `callback_url` (vendor: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Keyframe typing is the distinctive control: each of `frame0` and `frame1` is either `{"type": "image", "url": ...}` or `{"type": "generation", "id": ...}`, so a prior generation can be a boundary frame by id without re uploading. Extend continues from the end of an existing generation, reverse extend generates the footage leading up to one, and interpolate creates the transition between two generations. "Extend is currently supported only for generated videos. Please make sure the generation is in `completed` state" (vendor: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* No seed parameter appears anywhere in the Luma video generation API doc (vendor, confirmed absence: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20). Determinism on Luma therefore rests on keyframes, as it does on Higgsfield.
* Ray3.2 adds up to 16 keyframes per clip, a 360p Draft tier, resolutions 360p through 1080p, and per mode duration ceilings (5 to 10 seconds for text to video and image to video, up to 20 seconds for Modify Video, 12 to 16 seconds for Reframe) (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/ray, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 5. Documented best practices

* Runway iteration ladder (vendor): start minimal, add one element per retry, in the order subject motion, then camera motion, then scene motion, then style. "Adding one new element at a time will help you identify which additions improve your video, understand how different elements interact, and more effectively troubleshoot unexpected results" (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20). This matches the Cinema canon's one variable per retry rule exactly.
* Runway cost ladder (vendor): explore in Gen-4 Turbo at 5 credits per second, finalize in Gen-4 at 12 (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). Same preview cheap, finalize expensive shape as the Higgsfield 720p then 1080p workflow.
* Runway camera vocabulary (vendor): the Camera Terms reference defines and demonstrates the full working set: shot sizes (macro, extreme close up, close up, medium, full, wide, extreme wide, establishing), angles (aerial, high angle, low angle, bird's eye, worm's eye, over the shoulder, POV), compositions (leading lines, frame within frame, symmetrical, negative space), movements (pan, tilt, dolly, push in, pull back, truck, tracking, pedestal, crane, orbit, arc, zoom, crash zoom, whip pan, handheld, steadicam, gimbal, static), and focus techniques (deep, soft, rack, shallow). Combining terms is encouraged: "you can mix complex camera angles, motion, composition, and other elements to create your unique shot", and "When prompting for dramatic motion that moves through the frame, describe what's in view or what gets revealed at each phase of the movement" (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/46749315925395-Camera-Terms-Prompts-Examples, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Runway timestamp prompting (vendor, current doctrine): pair a natural language scene prompt with a bracketed time track. The guide's full example time track, verbatim:

  "[00:00 through 00:02] looking away, then turns towards camera
  [00:02 through 00:03] rapidly crash zoom to closely frame his eyes
  [00:03 through 00:04] black eyes squint
  [00:04 through 00:07] brow slowly furrows"

  "Though it may not be perfectly precise, it effectively guides the general sequence and timing", and each window must be long enough for the action to unfold naturally: for someone walking across a room, "setting timestamps 00:00 through 00:00.5 wouldn't give enough time for the action to unfold naturally—a more realistic timeframe might be 00:00 to 00:03" (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/42460036199443-Text-to-Video-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Runway keyword versus natural language (vendor): "Both work, but natural language usually gives you more control. When you write in full sentences, you provide context that helps guide exactly how elements appear in your scene." Keywords set "a general direction—think of them as suggestions" whose incorporation "may vary between generations" (same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Runway prompt length (vendor): "There's no ideal prompt length—focus on clarity rather than word count. Overly long prompts can create conflicting requests and may limit the creative freedom that produces surprising, dynamic shots." Text to video prompts run longer than image to video prompts because they must carry visuals and motion both (same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Runway mode choice (vendor): text to video "works well when exact character or scene consistency isn't the priority—like creating B-roll, stock effects, or background plates", and "Because Text to Video isn't constrained to a specific starting scene (like Image to Video is), it can handle complex motion sequences more effectively." Image to video is the consistency mode (same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Luma iteration context (vendor): within a Dream Machine board the system "remembers your earlier generations and builds upon them", and the Modify feature accepts deltas like "Make the colors warmer and add more trees" (https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/best-practices, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Luma keyframe craft (vendor): supply a text prompt alongside keyframes to "guide the transition or add specific details (e.g., 'transition from sunset to starry night')" (https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/how-to-use-keyframes, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Community, marked as anecdote: Ray2 practitioners converge on one action verb and one camera move per clip, leading with motion physics rather than style adjectives, because stacked actions soften output (community: https://filmart.ai/luma-ai-ray-2/ via search result summary, retrieved 2026-09-20). Same shape as the Seedance community's one main camera move per clip rule.

## 6. Known failure modes

* Negative phrasing backfires on Gen-4 (vendor documented): "Negative phrasing is not supported and may produce unpredictable or even opposite results" (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20). This is stronger than the Seedance situation, where negation is merely disputed; on Runway the vendor rules it out.
* Over describing the input image reduces motion on Gen-4 (vendor documented): reiterating image content "can lead to reduced motion or unexpected results" (same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Overloaded single prompts fail on Gen-4 (vendor documented): multiple scene changes, subject actions, or style shifts in one 5 or 10 second clip make the model "reconcile too many disparate elements or contradictory instructions" (same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Static cameras are hard everywhere (vendor documented, current doctrine): "Video models are designed to create immersive, high-fidelity motion, which can make achieving static shots challenging", worst on establishing and wide landscape shots. The vendor fix is positive reinforcement: add "The camera is entirely motionless for the duration of the scene, with movement only occurring from the subject." and describe how elements enter and exit the frame; for residual drift, stabilize in post (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/46749315925395-Camera-Terms-Prompts-Examples, retrieved 2026-09-20). Directly relevant to any locked camera canon shot attempted off catalog.
* Missing components need reinforcement, not rewrites (vendor documented): if a high angle does not appear, iterate "High angle looking down at a koi fish pond" rather than rewriting the prompt (https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/42460036199443-Text-to-Video-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Luma extend degrades quality (vendor documented): chaining Extend toward 30 seconds brings "a quality drop that can worsen with every extend" (https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20). Prefer single generations at target length.
* Luma vague prompt failures (vendor documented): official troubleshooting for bad Ray2 output is adjusting vague prompts, refreshing, or support contact; no region edit or repair path exists on the Dream Machine surface comparable to Seedance 2.5 region editing (https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Community reported, anecdote: Gen-3 era community guides reported that vague movement terms produce generic motion and that conflicting keyword stacks degrade coherence (community: https://filmart.ai/runway-gen-3-prompting/, retrieved 2026-09-20). Consistent with the vendor's specificity doctrine but not vendor confirmed for Gen-4.

## 7. Text rendering behavior

* Runway Gen-3 Alpha historically rendered on screen text supplied in quotation marks inside the prompt, and the retired guide carried text style keywords (bold, graffiti, neon, varsity, embroidery); this capability claim now rests only on community mirrors of the retired guide (community: https://filmart.ai/runway-gen-3-prompting/, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* The live Gen-4 prompting guide and spec article document no text rendering feature at all (vendor, confirmed absence across https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/39789879462419-Gen-4-Video-Prompting-Guide and https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). Do not promise in video text on Gen-4.
* Luma documents text rendering as a supported prompt request: specify "a poster with text that reads 'Dream Machine'" and optionally "ask for bold and unusual text styles" (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/best-practices, retrieved 2026-09-20). Reliability at video resolution is not quantified by the vendor.
* Cinema skill note: the canon forbids all generated text, so on any platform the working rule remains the exclusion block (no subtitles, captions, logos, watermarks, floating labels, or interface), with markings kept as physical surface properties (community, from the Seedance research: https://github.com/ZeroLu/awesome-seedance-2.5, per the prompting research file of 2026-09-18).

## 8. Reference and conditioning behavior

* Runway Gen-4 conditions on exactly one image, which is mandatory and becomes the output's first frame (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/37327109429011-Creating-with-Gen-4-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). There is no multi reference slot system, no end frame input, and no character, style, motion, or audio reference role on the Gen-4 video surface. Cross shot continuity is built by chaining: Use Current Frame promotes any frame of an output to the next generation's input image, which is the platform's native shared boundary frame mechanism (same URL). Fixed seed adds motion similarity across takes (same URL).
* Runway keyframe conditioning (start plus end frame) lived in Gen-3 Alpha and now lives in the Animate Frames app, per the retirement notice routing (vendor: https://help.runwayml.com/hc/en-us/articles/30586818553107-Gen-3-Alpha-Prompting-Guide, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Luma Ray2 conditions on start and end keyframes, each an uploaded image or a previous generation by id; extend, reverse extend, and interpolate between two generations are first class API operations (vendor: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20). Keyframes on the Dream Machine surface are "Available for Start and End Keyframes" for Ray2 (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/learning-hub/dream-machine-guide-ray2, retrieved 2026-09-20). API image inputs must be self hosted: "you should upload and use your own cdn image urls, currently this is the only way to pass an image" (vendor: https://docs.lumalabs.ai/docs/video-generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Ray3.2 widens conditioning to 16 keyframes per clip plus character reference and visual annotation drawn on frames (vendor: https://lumalabs.ai/ray and https://lumalabs.ai/ray3, retrieved 2026-09-20). This is the closest either vendor comes to Seedance style multi reference control, and it is still frame anchored rather than role labeled.

## 9. Seedance 2.5 delta: how prompting here differs from the skill's canon model

The Cinema skill's canon model is Seedance 2.5 on Higgsfield. Prompting Runway or Luma is a different discipline in six specific ways.

1. Prompt architecture inverts. Seedance 2.5 official guidance escalates to one labeled section block (GLOBAL STYLE, SCENE, CHARACTERS, LOCATION, FIRST FRAME AND BLOCKING, SHOT BY SHOT BREAKDOWN, OPTICS, PHYSICS, LIGHTING, AUDIO) with millisecond timecodes and an EVENT TRACK (vendor Higgsfield: https://higgsfield.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide, per the prompting research file of 2026-09-18). Runway prescribes the opposite: minimal motion first prompts, one element added per retry, and for Gen-4 the image carries everything visual. A canon style labeled block pasted into Gen-4 is exactly the "overly complex prompt" the vendor warns against. Luma sits between, wanting one conversational paragraph in its seven element order.
2. Multi shot lives in different places. Seedance 2.5 generates native multi shot sequences up to 30 seconds with "Hard cut" written at transitions (vendor ByteDance: https://seed.bytedance.com/en/blog/one-take-creation-flexible-referencing-introducing-seedance-2-5, per the research file). Gen-4 clips are 5 or 10 second single scenes by explicit design, and Ray2 is 5 or 10 seconds with degrading extends. On these platforms a sequence is cut together from separate generations chained by boundary frames, never prompted as one take.
3. Timecode prompting differs in kind. Seedance 2.5 takes absolute timecodes at millisecond precision as a core mechanism. Runway's current doctrine supports coarse bracketed windows ("[00:00 through 00:02]") with the caveat that they are approximate. Luma documents no timestamp prompting at all. Canon frame range prompts (FRAMES 010 TO 034 style) port to Runway only loosely and to Luma not at all.
4. Reference roles collapse into frames. Seedance 2.5 takes up to 50 role labeled references (30 image, 10 video, 10 audio) with `@character`, `@style`, `@motion`, `@audio` semantics on Higgsfield (vendor: same ByteDance blog and https://higgsfield.ai/creator-hub/help-center/ai-models/how-do-i-use-seedance, per the research file). Gen-4 accepts one image. Ray2 accepts two frames. Identity, style, and motion cannot be split across assets; everything must already be inside the conditioning frames. The canon's one reference one job discipline becomes: put the whole job into the boundary frame.
5. Negation policy is stricter. The Seedance evidence is split on negative prompts, with Higgsfield resolving toward positive locks plus targeted exclusions. Runway removes the ambiguity: negative phrasing is unsupported and can invert. Any HARD EXCLUSIONS block from the canon must be rewritten as positive locks before touching a Runway surface (turn "no camera movement" into "Locked camera. The camera remains still.").
6. Determinism levers differ. The Higgsfield video surface exposes no seed, making shared boundary frames the only determinism anchor (vendor: https://github.com/higgsfield-ai/cli/blob/main/MODELS.md, per the research file). Runway Gen-4 does expose a Fixed seed for motion similar retakes, a lever the canon workflow never gets to use. Luma exposes no seed and matches the canon's boundary frame doctrine, with the addition that a completed generation's id can serve directly as a keyframe.

Bottom line for the skill: these are capable off catalog alternates with their own platforms, credits, and prompting doctrines. Canon prompts do not transfer verbatim in either direction; a port to Runway means collapsing the labeled block into one motion sentence plus an input image, and a port to Luma means one conversational paragraph plus start and end keyframes.
