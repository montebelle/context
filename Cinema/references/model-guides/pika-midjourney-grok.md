## 1. Identity and version lineage

### 1.1 xAI Grok (Grok Imagine)

Grok Imagine is xAI's image and video generation product, first available to SuperGrok and Premium+ subscribers on iOS in August 2025, built on the lineage of Aurora, xAI's autoregressive text to image model launched 2024-12-09 (community: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grok_(chatbot), retrieved 2026-09-20). Community version tracking reports v0.1 in October 2025 with initial text to image and 16 FPS video, v0.5 in November 2025 adding image to video, and v0.9 in January 2026 adding 24 FPS output, native audio sync, and 10 plus aspect ratios (community, dates conflict with the August 2025 consumer launch above, treat as lore: https://www.cometapi.com/xai-launches-imagine-v0-9-what-it-is/, retrieved 2026-09-20).

The API surface is firmer. The Grok Imagine API launched 2026-01-28 as "a unified bundle of powerful APIs designed for end-to-end creative workflows" with native audio video generation (vendor: https://x.ai/news/grok-imagine-api, retrieved 2026-09-20). ## 3. Prompt structure the vendor prescribes

### 3.1 Grok

xAI publishes almost no prompt formula. The documented prescription is mode shaped, not sentence shaped: text to video takes `prompt` only; image to video is "Animate a still image with a text prompt. The source image becomes the starting point for the generated video."; reference to video takes `prompt` plus `reference_images` or `reference_audios`; first and last frame mode takes `image` plus `last_frame` (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/imagine and https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20). In pinned frame mode the prompt is optional; you can "let frames alone drive the clip or include guidance for motion and camera work" (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video, retrieved 2026-09-20). The extension endpoint's own worked example is the closest thing to vendor prompting style, plain scene direction: "The shot pans to an over the shoulder perspective. Calm controlled scene." (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/extension, retrieved 2026-09-20). Voice references are tagged in the prompt as `<AUDIO_0>`, `<AUDIO_1>`, `<AUDIO_2>` (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

Community structure for the gap the vendor leaves: a four element frame of motion verb, camera instruction, atmosphere, and mood or audio cue, with the rule "Not all four are required every time"; lead with what the image cannot show, because "The model sees the image. Redescription wastes prompt space."; use directional camera commands ("camera slowly pushes in") over adjectives ("cinematic and dynamic"); sequence at most three motion beats with commas; and match prompt length to source strength, 1 to 8 words on a strong image, 20 to 60 words for a directed aesthetic, avoiding the unclear 10 to 15 word middle (community: https://www.jxp.com/grok-imagine/blog/grok-imagine-video-1-5-prompt-guide, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 3.2 Pika

The official prompting guidance in the FAQ is thin and playful: "Include sexy keywords in your prompt, like bullet time, vertigo, timelapse or dolly down", with detail added to the keyword such as "bullet time 360 degrees" (vendor: https://pika.art/faq, retrieved 2026-09-20). Community practice fills in a six slot single paragraph structure: subject with distinguishing details, one continuous motion completable in five seconds, setting, shot size plus camera movement, lighting and color grade, and framing described in words because there is no aspect ratio parameter; in image to video, describe only what changes, since "The source image carries the subject." (community: https://prompt-architects.com/blog/255-pika-prompt-templates, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 3.3 Midjourney

Midjourney's vendor docs prescribe a workflow, not a formula. Two paths: automatic animation, where Midjourney analyzes the image and writes the motion prompt itself, and manual animation, where you write the motion prompt; motion intensity is a switch, not prose. Low motion is "more likely to result in still scenes, low camera motion, slow motion, or subtle character movements" and high motion is "more likely to result in big camera motions and larger character movements, however it may also create unrealistic or glitchy movements" (vendor: https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). Community guidance for the manual motion prompt: describe change over time with camera verbs (orbit, push in, tilt up, pan, track, crane), keep under Discord's 220 character limit, and do not restate what the starting image already shows (community: https://superduperai.co/blog/midjourney-v1-video and https://geekycuriosity.substack.com/p/how-to-write-midjourney-video-prompts, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 4. Parameters that change output

### 4.1 Grok on Higgsfield (live catalog reads, models_explore get, retrieved 2026-09-20)

* grok_video: `duration` 1 to 15 seconds, default 5. Aspect ratios 16:9, 9:16, 1:1. Media input: image with role start_image only. Tags include `audio`, `text-to-video`, `image-to-video`. No resolution parameter is exposed on this id.
* grok_video_v15: `resolution` 480p, 720p, or 1080p, default 720p; `duration` 2 to 15 seconds, default 5. Media input: image with roles start_image, image_references, and audio_references. The catalog lists an empty aspect ratio array for this id, so aspect selection is either absent or inherited; confirm in product before assuming a ratio can be forced. Tags include `cinematic`, `reference`, `audio-reference`, `start-frame`, `physics`, `camera-motion`, `preview`.
* grok_image: `resolution` 1k or 2k, default 1k; `mode` std or quality, default std. Aspect ratios 1:1, auto, 1:2, 2:1, 3:2, 2:3, 4:3, 3:4, 16:9, 9:16. Media input: image with role image_references.
* grok_image_2_0: `resolution` 1k or 2k, default 1k; `quality` low or medium, default medium. Same aspect ratios and media roles as grok_image.

### 4.2 Grok on the xAI API (for cross checking what the model itself supports)

Duration 1 to 15 seconds, video editing capped at 8.7 seconds; resolutions 480p (default), 720p, 1080p, with native 1080p on grok-imagine-video-1.5 for text and image to video while reference to video is capped at 720p; aspect ratios 1:1, 16:9 (default), 9:16, 4:3, 3:4, 3:2, 2:3; audio on by default, "Generated videos include an audio track by default. Pass `generate_audio=False` to request a silent video.", with up to three preset voices via voice_id (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20). Image generation configures "output count (up to 10 images per request), aspect ratio, resolution, and response format" (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/imagine, retrieved 2026-09-20). Extension: `duration` "controls the length of the extended portion only, not the total output" (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/extension, retrieved 2026-09-20). Editing ignores duration, aspect ratio, and resolution because "the output inherits these properties from the input video, capped at 720p" (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/editing, retrieved 2026-09-20). No seed, cfg, or negative prompt parameter appears anywhere in the fetched video docs (vendor, confirmed absence: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20), so the determinism situation matches the Higgsfield Seedance surface: conditioning images, not seeds, are the only repeatability lever.

### 4.3 Pika (own platform)

Durations: 5 or 10 seconds on Pika 2.2 standard generations, 5 seconds on everything else, and up to 25 seconds through Pikaframes keyframe sequences. Resolutions: 720p or 1080p on 2.2, 1080p on 2.1, 720p on 1.5 and Turbo (vendor: https://pika.art/faq, retrieved 2026-09-20). Community reports a genuine `negative_prompt` field on the API surface, used for exclusions such as "text, watermark, distorted hands, extra fingers, camera shake, oversaturated colour", and no aspect ratio parameter at all, frame shape being described in prompt words (community: https://prompt-architects.com/blog/255-pika-prompt-templates, retrieved 2026-09-20). Pikaffects are a fixed effect menu (community counts 62 image effects and 9 video effects) that accepts no text prompt; the source image is the only control (community: same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 4.4 Midjourney (own platform)

"Video generations are only compatible with video-specific parameters: --motion low, --motion high, --raw, --loop, --end, and --bs #." and "Any image parameters used to generate your original image will be automatically removed when generating videos" (vendor: https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). `--bs` sets batch size (default four clips per job, reducible to 1 or 2); `--loop` reuses the starting frame as the end frame; `--end` sets a different ending image; resolution is fixed low, "Midjourney videos are generated in 480p quality, also called Standard Definition (SD)", with 720p HD only on Pro and Mega plans (vendor: same URL plus https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32859204029709-Parameter-List, retrieved 2026-09-20). Duration: 5 second base, "Feel free to extend your video up to 4 times, until it reaches 21 seconds, which is the maximum length available.", each extension adding about 4 seconds and costing the same GPU time as an initial generation, with Extend Auto reusing the original prompt and Extend Manual letting you edit it (vendor: https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 5. Documented best practices

* Grok, vendor: pick the mode by intent. Use image to video to lock the opening frame; use reference to video when elements should appear "without locking the first frame"; use pinned first and last frames when both boundary images are known; use editing for surgical changes, which delivers "high-fidelity edits with strong scene preservation, modifying only what you ask for while keeping the rest of the video intact."; chain the extension endpoint for length beyond 15 seconds, and branch multiple concurrent edits from one intermediate result when exploring variants (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video, https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/editing, https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/extension, retrieved 2026-09-20). Download outputs promptly: "Videos are returned as temporary URLs." (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Grok, community: one coherent camera path per shot; physical event verbs over aesthetic adjectives; for a locked frame write "camera not moving" rather than "stable camera" because "Negative instructions lock the frame more reliably."; never request 4K or resolution in the prompt, it is ignored and wastes words (community: https://www.jxp.com/grok-imagine/blog/grok-imagine-video-1-5-prompt-guide, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Pika, community: write one dense paragraph without line breaks, roughly 110 words calibrated on Pika's own worked example; put duration in the request settings, never in the sentence ("writing 'a ten second shot' into your prompt accomplishes nothing"); in Pikaframes write one prompt that is true of every transition between the 2 to 5 keyframes; in Pikaswaps name what to replace and what must stay unchanged (community: https://prompt-architects.com/blog/255-pika-prompt-templates, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Midjourney, vendor: start from a strong image, choose low motion by default and high motion only when the shot needs large movement, and use the web Loop checkbox or an Ending Frame image for boundary control (vendor: https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). Community: chain Extend Manual with a new action each time to build a narrative across the 21 second ceiling, and mask the 480p limit with "macro shots, silhouettes, high contrast" compositions (community: https://superduperai.co/blog/midjourney-v1-video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 6. Known failure modes

* Grok, vendor documented: mode and model mismatches fail with `failed_precondition` ("Unsupported model/mode combination"); generation is asynchronous with statuses pending, done, expired, and failed, and an expired request loses the output; moderation is real and surfaces as a `respect_moderation` boolean on responses (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Grok, community reported: three failure classes account for most bad clips, prompt overload, content policy refusals, and the hard 15 second cap; moderation can fail late because upscaling adds realism and pushes a clip past the risk threshold that its lower resolution draft passed (community: https://aiinsightsnews.net/grok-video-moderated/ and https://aihubinfo.github.io/familypro/en/blog/grok-imagine-video-prompt-guide-50-examples-2026/, retrieved 2026-09-20). Aesthetic adjectives with no actionable instruction, redescribing the frame, contradictory camera paths, and weak motion verbs each produce flat or unpredictable motion, with the fixes being physical events, motion first phrasing, one path per shot, and specific verbs (community: https://www.jxp.com/grok-imagine/blog/grok-imagine-video-1-5-prompt-guide, retrieved 2026-09-20). Lip sync from a still is inconsistent: "Build dialogue-driven content on a text-to-video model instead." (community: same URL, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Pika, vendor documented: duration and resolution ceilings per model as in section 4.3; free tier commercial use is restricted (vendor: https://pika.art/faq, retrieved 2026-09-20). Community reported: Pika is the wrong choice when a shot needs more than five seconds, an exact aspect ratio, or an effect outside the fixed Pikaffects menu; effects deform the subject, so source stills need subject isolation and headroom for the deformation (community: https://prompt-architects.com/blog/255-pika-prompt-templates, retrieved 2026-09-20).
* Midjourney, vendor documented: high motion "may also create unrealistic or glitchy movements" (vendor: https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). Community reported: chaotic motion is tamed with `--motion low` or one primary motion element; loops fail on linear movements with clear start and end points, so prompt "seamless loop" content; 480p artifacts are masked, not fixed (community: https://superduperai.co/blog/midjourney-v1-video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 7. Text rendering behavior

None of the three vendors documents in video text rendering in the pages fetched for this guide (vendor, confirmed absence: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, https://pika.art/faq, https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, all retrieved 2026-09-20). Community signal is indirect: Pika practitioners put "text, watermark" first in the negative prompt field, implying unwanted text emergence is common enough to exclude by default (community: https://prompt-architects.com/blog/255-pika-prompt-templates, retrieved 2026-09-20), and Midjourney video inherits whatever text the starting image carries, so legible text must be won in the still image first and can degrade at 480p (community: https://superduperai.co/blog/midjourney-v1-video, retrieved 2026-09-20). For Grok image models no vendor claim about typography was found either way. Treat all three as unverified for text and follow the skill's standing rule: keep required text out of generation and add it in post, and for OASYS style work forbid emergent text explicitly in the prompt.

## 8. Reference and conditioning behavior

### 8.1 Grok (the richest conditioning surface of the three)

Five distinct conditioning modes, vendor defined (https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation and https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video, retrieved 2026-09-20):

1. Start frame: `image` alone is image to video; the source image is the literal first frame.
2. References: `reference_images` "incorporate specific people, objects, clothing, or other visual elements without locking the first frame", stated as "useful for virtual try-on, product placement, character-consistent storytelling, and voice identity." No maximum reference image count is stated in the fetched pages (vendor, confirmed absence, 2026-09-20); the 5 source image limit documented for multi image editing is an image endpoint limit, not a video one.
3. Pinned boundaries: on grok-imagine-video-1.5, `image` plus `last_frame` pins exact first and last frames, and "The video ends arriving on that image rather than re-rendering it as a reference." Combining `image` with references makes that image "the pinned first frame" of a reference to video run (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/image-to-video, retrieved 2026-09-20). This is a true last frame contract of the kind the OASYS boundary frame method wants.
4. Extension: continues an existing video from its last frame via `/v1/videos/extensions` (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/extension, retrieved 2026-09-20).
5. Audio identity: up to three preset voices via `reference_audios`, tagged in prompt as `<AUDIO_0>` and so on; "Voice references with your own audio files are available to trusted partners, on request." (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/reference-to-video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

On Higgsfield only grok_video_v15 exposes the reference roles (start_image, image_references, audio_references); grok_video takes start_image alone (live catalog reads, 2026-09-20). The catalog does not surface a last frame slot on either id (live catalog read, confirmed absence, 2026-09-20), so whether Higgsfield passes last frame pinning through is unknown; verify in product before relying on it. Image side: grok_image and grok_image_2_0 both accept image_references, matching the API's edit path of "up to 5 source images in a single request for combining subjects, transferring styles, and composing scenes" (vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/imagine, retrieved 2026-09-20).

### 8.2 Pika

Conditioning is feature routed rather than slot routed: Pikaframes takes 2 to 5 keyframes and interpolates transitions (up to 25 seconds); Pikaswaps takes a video plus a prompt and optionally a reference image to swap into the masked area; Pikadditions takes a video plus an image or prompt for the inserted element (vendor: https://pika.art/faq, retrieved 2026-09-20). There is no documented multi reference identity system comparable to Seedance labels or Grok reference images.

### 8.3 Midjourney

The starting image is the only strong conditioning input, plus one optional `--end` image; identity across clips is held by reusing the same starting frame or by Midjourney's image side character reference tools before animation, not by any video side reference slot (vendor: https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/37460773864589-Video, retrieved 2026-09-20). Extensions continue from the last frame, which is the same shared boundary logic the skill uses elsewhere (community workflow confirmation: https://superduperai.co/blog/midjourney-v1-video, retrieved 2026-09-20).

## 9. Seedance 2.5 delta

How prompting these families differs from the skill's canon model, Seedance 2.5 on Higgsfield, as documented in the OASYS research file (/Users/mbps/Documents/GitHub/OASYS/docs/from-desert-to-oasys/from-desert-to-oasys-prompting-research-v1.md) and its cited sources.

* Prompt architecture. Seedance 2.5 rewards a long labeled section block (GLOBAL STYLE, SCENE, CHARACTERS, LOCATION, FIRST FRAME AND BLOCKING, SHOT BY SHOT BREAKDOWN, OPTICS, PHYSICS, LIGHTING, AUDIO) with millisecond timecodes and an EVENT TRACK (official Higgsfield guidance: https://higgsfield.ai/blog/seedance-2-5-prompting-guide, retrieved 2026-09-20 via the research file). None of these three families wants that. Grok takes short motion first prose and expresses structure through API modes and slots, not prompt labels; community guidance actively warns that long redescription wastes the prompt (community: https://www.jxp.com/grok-imagine/blog/grok-imagine-video-1-5-prompt-guide, retrieved 2026-09-20). Midjourney reduces prompting to a motion sentence plus switches. Pika wants one dense paragraph under about 110 words. Porting a Seedance canon prompt verbatim to any of them would be counterproductive; strip it to the motion beats and camera path.
* Duration and shot planning. Seedance 2.5 generates up to 30 seconds with internal hard cuts planned in the prompt. Grok caps a generation at 15 seconds and reaches length by chained extensions; Midjourney caps at 5 plus four 4 second extensions (21 seconds); Pika caps at 5 or 10 seconds, 25 via Pikaframes. On all three, multi shot sequences live across multiple generations stitched at boundaries, never inside one prompt, so the Seedance device of writing "Hard cut" inside a prompt has no equivalent.
* References. Seedance 2.5 takes up to 50 labeled references addressed in prompt (@Image1 style), with the working advice to use a small role scoped set. Grok reference images are unlabeled in prompt (only audio gets `<AUDIO_0>` tags), with no documented count ceiling in the fetched docs; role scoping must therefore happen through mode choice (start frame versus reference versus pinned frames) rather than through per asset prompt labels. Pika and Midjourney have no comparable reference system at all.
* Boundary frames and determinism. Neither Seedance on Higgsfield nor Grok exposes a seed (research file section 5.4; vendor confirmed absence for Grok: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20), so both surfaces share the same determinism strategy: exact start frames, and where available exact end frames. Grok 1.5's pinned last frame ("The video ends arriving on that image") is a stronger vendor contract than the Seedance help center's text based FIRST FRAME AND BLOCKING boundary, and Midjourney's `--end` offers the same idea at low fidelity. If a shot ever had to move off canon Seedance while preserving the shared boundary frame method, grok_video_v15 is the only catalog id in this guide that structurally supports it, pending in product confirmation that Higgsfield exposes the last frame slot.
* Audio. Seedance 2.5 and Grok both generate audio by default and both need it explicitly disabled for silent work (Grok: `generate_audio=False`, vendor: https://docs.x.ai/developers/model-capabilities/video/generation, retrieved 2026-09-20). Pika and Midjourney video are silent surfaces by default in the fetched docs.
* Negative prompting. The Seedance canon resolves the negation debate with positive locks plus targeted exclusions in prose. Pika is the outlier here: it has a real `negative_prompt` field (community: https://prompt-architects.com/blog/255-pika-prompt-templates, retrieved 2026-09-20), so exclusions move out of the main prompt entirely. Grok and Midjourney have no negative field; Grok community practice even uses negation inside prose deliberately ("camera not moving") for frame locking.
